fetch electricity data for the past year at least OR the past month if tight on token usage.
use it to come up with a mathematical model of the peak usage times, rates, demand, etc, and find patterns in it.
find which pattern corresponds to the cheapest cost and least demand.
let: pattern found, frequency: daily. then overlap this time frame with grid usage.
let there be a budget limit for grid usage. then pull max optimized power from the grid as follows:
let there be a unified big reservoir for the incoming grid power. this will act as a big buffer. its capacity and specifics will be optimized later. rn its a conceptual obj.
then let a_i be different forms of energy that can be semantically and intuitively thought of as being subsets of E in some parameter.
then instead of directing all incoming grid power to the main reservoir we can redirect it directly to the reservoir OF the closest a_i that its gonna be used for.
downsides: energy is still going to be lost. for exmaple: thermal reservoir loses heat, caprs self discharge, batteries degrade over time.
"peak grid power": at a given time range t, it is the maximum power pulled from the microgrid-grid system- ex: solar power is down, so the reservoir is being used by everyone for EVs, AC systems, machines, etc.
physicist approach- model the simplest possible model of the problem and solve it first. then add on additional functionality.

Simplest Model:
Setup{
1 House, 1 solar panel, 1 connection to grid, 1 EV charger, 1 battery (unified reservoir for this system), suitable plausible and simplest arbitrary N number of household appliances represented by x_i.
Each appliance has a priority rating bel [0,100] ∩ W.
}

Current core ideas:


1. E consumption equation must be something like: 
E_available(t) - ∑ E_(α_i x_i)  = E_crit(t)+E_crit_buffer(arbitrary) -> Baseline condition.
If LHS > RHS, LHS-RHS goes to reservoirs.
E_available is a piecewise function of time which may or may not be parametric, and serves as the available incoming energy. It can be solar+grid, only grid, or only solar, depending on certain conditions.
Using a linear combination approach where α_i is the respective priority score of each x_i, where x_i is the i'th household appliance. So α_i is the weight.
SO E_(α_i x_i) means energy consumption of appliance x_i with its respective priority score α_i.
x_i does NOT include the critical loads.
E_crit(t) is an arbitrary function of time (and it also has the possibility to be a constant for simpler conditions and approximations).
E_crit(t) is the resultant energy consumption required by the critical loads.
E_crit_buffer(t) is a function of some suitable parameter(s). could be piecewise and parametric in its parameters.


2. Luxury thresholds:
First:
E_in = E_consumed
Why- Because below this thershold, we would be effectively disregarding the lowest priority appliances.
So, to have all appliances have exactly enough E_in to consume is the first luxury.

Second:
E_in > E_consumed -> slightly >, where E_in-E_cons=E_Conceptual_Reservoir_Buffer
E_Conceptual_Reservoir_Buffer is a conceptual buffer which in reality, encompasses the distribution of E_in-E_consumed among all reservoirs according to the algorithm.

Third:
E_in >> E_consumed
